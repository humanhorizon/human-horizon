## What it looks like at work

Real adoption has a few visible signs:

- AI is built into specific workflows, such as quotes, reports or replies, not left as an optional extra.
- People reach for it without being reminded.
- Managers review AI-assisted work as part of normal sign-off, with a person still checking the output.
- There are clear rules on approved tools and data, and people know them.
- Use holds steady once the early excitement has gone.

The usual barriers are human, not technical. Rules are unclear, so people hold back. Some quietly worry about their jobs. Training used generic examples. Nobody has time to practise. Nobody owns making the change stick.

## An example

An events company bought AI licences for every staff member. Most licences sat unused. The company changed approach. It picked two recurring tasks, post-event reports and supplier briefs, and rebuilt them so AI drafts each one from the team's notes and a person checks and finalises it. The team trained on those two tasks. Managers then asked for reports in the new format. Use followed the work.

## AI adoption vs AI implementation

Implementation is building and setting up: the tools, the workflows, the access. Adoption is people choosing to use them as part of their normal day. A company can implement well and still fail to adopt. Both are needed.
