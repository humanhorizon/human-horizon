## What it looks like at work

In a small or mid-sized company, AI governance does not need a committee. It needs a few clear answers, written down and known by everyone:

- **Approved tools:** which AI tools and accounts staff may use for work.
- **Data rules:** what may go into each tool, and what never should, such as NRIC numbers or confidential client files.
- **Checking:** which outputs need a person's review, and who signs off before anything goes to a client or changes a record.
- **Ownership:** who decides on new tools and uses, and who people ask when unsure.
- **Problems:** what staff should do if something goes wrong, such as data shared by mistake or a wrong answer sent.
- **A list of AI workflows**, so the company knows where AI is in use.

Keep it proportionate. A tool that drafts internal notes needs lighter rules than one that touches customer data or money.

## Singapore frameworks worth knowing

Singapore agencies have published several frameworks and guidelines that can help. Useful ones include:

- IMDA and PDPC's Model AI Governance Framework. A [one-page primer](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Resource-for-Organisation/AI/Primer-for-2nd-edition-of-AI-Gov-Framework.pdf) on its second edition covers internal governance, how much human involvement to build in, operations management and communicating with stakeholders.
- The [Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/resources/mgf-gen-ai/), released in May 2024, with nine dimensions from accountability and security to using AI for the public good.
- IMDA's [Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/news-and-events/media-room/media-releases/2026/01/factsheet-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai.pdf), for organisations deploying AI agents.
- [AI Verify](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/what-is-ai-verify/), a testing framework based on 11 AI governance principles.
- PDPC's [Advisory Guidelines on the Use of Personal Data in AI Recommendation and Decision Systems](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/advisory-guidelines-on-the-use-of-personal-data-in-ai-recommendation-and-decision-systems.pdf), issued on 1 March 2024. They are advisory and not legally binding, but they explain how the PDPA applies.

## An example

A 30-person engineering consultancy writes a one-page AI rulebook. It lists two approved tools on company accounts, bans client drawings and personal data from any other tool, requires an engineer to check any AI-drafted technical content before it goes out, and names the operations manager as the person to ask. Staff go through it in a short session using their own examples.
