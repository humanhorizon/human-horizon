## How it works

With AI, the technology is often the easy part. The harder part is people. Most change management for AI comes down to a handful of steps:

- **Explain the why.** Say plainly what problem the change solves, such as late nights at month end or slow replies to customers.
- **Answer the job question honestly.** Many people wonder whether AI is coming for their role. Say what will change in their work and what will not. Silence lets the worry grow.
- **Involve the people who do the work.** They know where the real pain is, and they will trust a process they helped shape.
- **Train on real tasks.** People commit to a new way when they can see it working on their own work.
- **Let managers lead by example.** When managers use and review AI-assisted work, others follow.
- **Make the new way the easy way.** Build it into the templates, systems and sign-offs people already use.
- **Listen and adjust.** Early problems are useful. Fix them in the open.

## An example

The finance team at a distribution company was worried AI would replace them. The finance manager explained that the goal was to cut month-end overtime, not jobs. She asked the team which tasks they disliked most. They chose invoice matching. Together they redesigned it so AI suggests matches and staff check every exception before anything is posted. The team helped write the checking rules, and they now run the process themselves.
