## What it looks like at work

A good policy fits on one or two pages and uses examples from the team's real work. It usually covers:

- **Approved tools and accounts.** Which tools staff may use, and that work happens on company accounts, not personal ones.
- **Data rules.** What must never go into an unapproved tool. PDPC's [guide to the PDPA](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Resource-for-Individuals/what-you-need-to-know-about-pdpa-v1-0.pdf) gives examples of personal data such as full names, NRIC or passport numbers, photos, mobile numbers, personal email addresses and residential addresses. Add your own confidential items, such as client files, pricing and unreleased financials.
- **Checking.** Every AI-assisted output is reviewed by a person before it is sent, filed or acted on. Say who signs off on client-facing work.
- **Honesty with clients.** When and how to tell clients that AI was used, if your clients or contracts expect it.
- **Questions and mistakes.** Who to ask, and what to do straight away if something is shared by mistake.

Under the PDPA, organisations must develop policies and practices to meet their obligations. PDPC's [Advisory Guidelines on the Use of Personal Data in AI Recommendation and Decision Systems](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/advisory-guidelines-on-the-use-of-personal-data-in-ai-recommendation-and-decision-systems.pdf) point to Section 12 for this, and say written policies help organisations show how they use personal data responsibly. An AI acceptable use policy is one practical piece of that.

## An example

A tuition centre group writes a one-page AI policy. Teachers may use the approved AI assistant to draft worksheets and parent updates. Student names, contact details and results stay out of any tool that has not been approved. Every parent update is read by the teacher before sending. The centre manager is the contact for questions. Teachers learn it in a short session using their own worksheets.

## AI acceptable use policy vs AI governance

The policy is the part staff read. AI governance is the wider system behind it: who decides on tools, how workflows are designed and checked, and how problems are handled.
