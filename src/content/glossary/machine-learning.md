## How it works

Ordinary software does exactly what it is told. Someone writes the rules, and the program follows them. Machine learning flips that. The system is given many past examples, such as invoices marked paid on time or paid late, and it finds the patterns itself. The result is a model that can score a new case it has not seen before.

The US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) glossary defines machine learning as ["the development and use of computer systems that adapt and learn from data with the goal of improving accuracy"](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/ml) (page read 16 Nov 2026).

It is one way of building [artificial intelligence](/insights/glossary/artificial-intelligence-ai), and many of the AI tools in use at work today are built with it. Common forms at work:

- **Sorting:** labelling things from past examples, such as spam or not spam.
- **Predicting:** estimating a number, such as next month's demand.
- **Spotting the unusual:** flagging a payment that looks unlike the rest.
- **Generating:** a [large language model](/insights/glossary/large-language-model-llm) is a machine learning model trained on very large amounts of text, and it sits behind many [generative AI](/insights/glossary/generative-ai) tools.

A model is only as good as its examples. If the data is thin, out of date or not like the real cases it will face, the results will be weak. Testing a model against cases where you already know the answer, and having a person review decisions that matter, reduces the risk. It does not remove it.

An [AI agent](/insights/glossary/ai-agent) is one step further: given a goal and some tools, it uses a model to work out the steps, then takes them through those tools.

## An example

Imagine a distribution firm with a small finance team. It trains a model on two years of its own invoices, labelled paid on time or paid late. Each Monday the model scores the open invoices and lists those most likely to be late. The finance manager reads the list, uses what she knows about each customer, and chases the risky ones early. The model does the sorting. She makes the call. This is an illustration, not a real client.
