## What it looks like at work

A prompt can be one line: "Summarise this email." It can also be a full brief that says who the reader is, what you need, the format, an example of good output and the document to work from.

A strong work prompt usually covers five things:

- **The task:** what you want done.
- **The context:** who it is for and why it matters.
- **The material:** the notes, document or data to use.
- **The format:** length, structure and tone.
- **The checks:** what to flag if the AI is unsure or the material is missing something.

AI tools do not know your clients, your house style or your standards unless you tell them. A short prompt leaves the tool to guess. A complete one gives it what a new colleague would need.

Teams that use AI well save their best prompts as shared templates. A strong instruction is written once, tested, and then reused by everyone who does the same task.

## An example

An associate at a small accounting firm needs to chase clients for missing documents. Her first prompt, "Write a reminder email", produces something generic. Her second prompt adds the client's name, the list of missing items, the deadline from the engagement letter, the firm's usual friendly but firm tone and a limit of 120 words. The draft is close to ready. She checks the list and the date against the client file, then sends it. The firm saves the prompt as a template for the whole team.
