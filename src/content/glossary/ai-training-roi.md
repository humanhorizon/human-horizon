## How it works

ROI has two sides.

**The costs** include training fees, the time staff spend in sessions and practising, any new tool licences and set-up work.

**The returns** come from changes in specific tasks:

- hours saved per task, multiplied by how often the task happens and the cost of that time
- faster turnaround, such as quotes or replies going out sooner
- fewer errors or less rework
- capacity: more work handled by the same team

To make the numbers believable:

1. **Pick the tasks first.** Choose the recurring work the training is meant to change.
2. **Measure a baseline** before training: time per task, volume and error rate.
3. **Measure again afterwards**, on the same tasks.
4. **Include checking time.** A person still reviews AI output, and that time counts.
5. **Ask where the time went.** Hours saved only become a return if they are used for something valuable.

Feedback forms tell you whether people enjoyed the session. They do not tell you whether the work changed.

## An example

A wholesale company trains five sales coordinators to draft quotes with AI. Before the training, they time a sample of quotes and note how many need correcting. Afterwards, they time the same kind of quotes again, including the minutes each coordinator spends checking the AI's draft. The company multiplies the time saved by the number of quotes and the cost of that time, then compares the result with the training fees and the hours spent in training.
