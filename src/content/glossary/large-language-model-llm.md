## How it works

An LLM learns from huge collections of written text. During training it practises one task again and again: guessing the next word, or more precisely the next small chunk of text, called a token. Doing that across a vast number of examples gives it a working grasp of grammar, common facts, writing styles and how ideas usually connect. Most models are then trained further to follow instructions and give helpful answers.

When you give it a prompt, it builds its answer one token at a time. Each time, it picks a likely next token, given everything before it. It does not look things up in a database unless it has been connected to one. It does not remember your past chats unless the tool is set up to keep them.

"Large" refers to the size of the model and the amount of text it learned from. Larger models tend to handle harder and longer tasks, but they usually cost more to run.

## An example

A freight forwarder in Singapore receives booking requests by email, each in a different format. An LLM reads every email and pulls the shipper, consignee, ports, cargo details and requested dates into a draft booking in the company's system. An operations executive compares the draft with the original email, fixes anything wrong and confirms it. Retyping turns into checking, and the queue moves faster.

## LLM vs generative AI

Generative AI is the wider family of tools that create text, images, audio or video. An LLM is the kind of generative AI that works mainly with language. Most AI assistants used in offices today are built on LLMs.
