The OECD [describes an AI system](https://oecd.ai/en/wonk/definition) as a machine-based system that infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions, and notes that systems vary in how autonomous and adaptive they are.

## What does AI look like at work?

A tool that drafts a reply to a customer email is AI. So is a tool that reads an invoice and pulls out the date and amount, or one that sorts enquiries by topic. A calculator or a spreadsheet formula is not, because it follows steps someone wrote out in full.

## How is AI different from automation?

Automation does the same steps the same way every time. AI can handle input that varies, such as a free-text email. The two work well together: AI reads or drafts, and automation moves the result to the right place. See [Automation](/insights/glossary/automation).

## Why check what AI produces?

Many AI tools, including those that write text, predict what is likely to fit. That is not the same as knowing what is true, so a person should check anything that matters. For how it works in more detail, read [How does AI work?](/insights/how-does-ai-work).
