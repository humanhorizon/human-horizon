## How it works

Traditional automation follows fixed rules. "When a form is submitted, add a row to the sheet and email the sales team." It works well when the input is tidy and predictable.

Much of an SME's real work is not tidy. Requests arrive as emails, PDFs, photos and voice notes. AI extends automation to that messy material. It can read an email, pull out the details, sort the request and draft the next document. The fixed rules then carry the result to the right place.

A sound automated workflow is designed in three layers:

- **Rules** for the steps that are always the same.
- **AI** for the steps that need reading, sorting or drafting.
- **A person** at the points where judgement matters, or where anything goes to a client, changes a record or moves money.

Start by mapping the process as it really runs today, including the workarounds. Automating a muddled process only makes the muddle faster.

## An example

A corporate secretarial firm onboards new clients by email. Documents arrive in every format. Now AI reads each document, fills in the onboarding checklist, flags anything missing and drafts a chaser email for the gaps. A corporate secretarial executive checks the extracted details against the source documents and approves each chaser before it goes. The team stops retyping and spends its time on the files that need real attention.

## Workflow automation vs AI agent

Workflow automation follows a set path that someone designed. An AI agent decides some of its own steps as it goes. Many good workflows combine both: a fixed path, with AI handling specific steps and a person checking at the key points.
