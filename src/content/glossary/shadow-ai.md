## What it looks like at work

Shadow AI is usually well meant. People find a tool that saves them time and start using it. Common forms include:

- personal chatbot accounts used for work emails, reports or contracts
- free browser extensions that read and rewrite what is on screen
- AI note-takers that join client calls
- free transcription, translation or summary websites

The risks are real even when the intent is good:

- **Data leaves your control.** Client details, staff records or pricing can end up in a tool whose terms nobody has read.
- **There is no record.** The company cannot see what was shared or what AI produced.
- **Output goes unchecked.** Nobody has agreed how AI-assisted work should be reviewed.
- **Quality varies.** Each person uses a different tool in a different way.

Under Singapore's PDPA, the company stays responsible. PDPC's [Advisory Guidelines on Key Concepts in the PDPA](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/important-terms-in-pdpa---ch-3-9-%28270717%29.pdf) say organisations remain responsible for employees' actions that breach the data protection provisions. PDPC's [Advisory Guidelines on the Use of Personal Data in AI Recommendation and Decision Systems](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/advisory-guidelines-on-the-use-of-personal-data-in-ai-recommendation-and-decision-systems.pdf) also note that Section 12 of the PDPA requires organisations to develop policies and practices to meet their obligations under the Act.

Banning AI rarely works. It tends to push use further out of sight. A better response is to offer approved tools, set clear and simple rules, train people, and give them someone to ask.

## An example

At a corporate services firm, several staff use a free website to transcribe and summarise client meetings. A manager notices when a summary arrives in an unfamiliar format. Instead of banning it, the firm sets up an approved tool on a business account, writes a one-page rule on what may be recorded and processed, and asks staff to check every summary against their notes before filing it.

## Shadow AI vs shadow IT

Shadow IT is any technology used without approval, such as a personal file-sharing account. Shadow AI is the AI version. It carries an extra risk, because staff often paste whole documents into it.
