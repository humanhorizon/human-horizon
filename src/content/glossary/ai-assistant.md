## What it looks like at work

AI assistants come in two main forms. Some are standalone chat tools, such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, where you type a request and get a reply. Others are built into software your team already uses, such as [Microsoft 365 Copilot](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/in-apps-for-work) in Word, Excel and Outlook, or [Gemini in Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/solutions/ai/), so the help appears inside your email, documents and spreadsheets.

Typical everyday uses include:

- turning rough notes into a clean email or report
- summarising a long document or meeting transcript
- drafting a first version of a proposal or policy
- explaining a formula or cleaning up a spreadsheet
- brainstorming options before a decision

The assistant does the first pass. The person using it stays responsible for what goes out.

Accounts matter too. The same assistant can come in personal and business versions, and the data terms and admin controls can differ between plans. It is worth checking the terms of the plan your team actually uses, and giving staff company accounts so work data stays under the company's control.

## An example

An account manager at a 15-person marketing agency finishes a client call with a page of rough notes. She asks the company's AI assistant to turn them into a meeting summary with an action list, owners and open questions. She reads the summary against her notes, corrects one action that was assigned to the wrong person and sends it to the client.

## AI assistant vs AI agent

An assistant responds to you, one request at a time. An agent works through several steps towards a goal by itself. Most teams start with an assistant and add agents for specific, well-understood workflows.
