## What it looks like at work

AI literacy is not coding. For most staff it comes down to a few habits:

- **Knowing the limits.** AI predicts likely answers and can be confidently wrong.
- **Asking well.** Giving clear instructions, context and the right material.
- **Checking.** Comparing the output with the source before it is used.
- **Protecting data.** Knowing what can go into which tool, and what never should.
- **Judging fit.** Knowing when AI helps, and when a task is better done by hand.

Managers need one more layer: how to review AI-assisted work, and how to decide where AI fits in their team's work.

Singapore's guidance treats this as part of using AI responsibly. IMDA's [factsheet on the Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/news-and-events/media-room/media-releases/2026/01/factsheet-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai.pdf) calls for training users to use agents responsibly and oversee them effectively, while making sure they keep their foundational skills.

## An example

Front desk staff at a group of clinics learn to use an approved AI tool to draft replies to appointment enquiries. Their training covers three rules. Never paste NRIC numbers or medical details into a tool that has not been approved. Check every draft against the clinic's actual schedule before sending. Pass anything clinical to a nurse. Replies go out faster, and the staff stay in charge of what is said.

## AI literacy vs AI skills

AI literacy is the shared foundation everyone needs. AI skills are the role-specific uses built on top, such as a finance team reconciling accounts or a sales team drafting quotes.
