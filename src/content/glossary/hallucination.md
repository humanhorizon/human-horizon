## How it works

Most AI writing tools are built on large language models. These models predict what text is likely to come next. They do not check facts the way a person looks something up. When the model lacks the right information, it can fill the gap with something that reads well but is not true.

Common forms at work include:

- figures, dates or prices that were never in the source
- references, quotes or links that do not exist
- a document misread or summarised with a detail added
- facts from one client or case mixed into another

You can reduce hallucinations. Give the tool the source material instead of asking from memory. Ask it to say "not found" when the answer is not in the material. Ask it to point to where each claim came from. Connect it to your own documents so it answers from them. These steps lower the risk but do not remove it.

The dependable safeguard is a person who checks the output against the source before it is used.

## An example

A sales coordinator at a trading company asks AI to draft a reply to a customer asking about returns. The draft mentions a "standard 14-day return window". The company has no such policy. Because the workflow requires the coordinator to check any terms against the company's terms of sale before sending, she spots it, corrects the reply and sends it. The team then adds the terms of sale to the tool's source material, so future drafts start from the real policy.
