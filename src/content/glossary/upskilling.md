## What it looks like at work

Good AI upskilling is built around jobs, not tools. It tends to share four features:

- **It is role-based.** A finance team learns different uses from a sales team.
- **It uses real tasks.** People practise on their own documents and recurring work, so the skill applies the moment they are back at their desk.
- **It continues after the session.** People need room to practise and someone to ask when they get stuck.
- **Managers take part.** When managers expect and review AI-assisted work, the new way becomes normal.

Generic courses often skip these. People enjoy the session, then go back to work that has not changed, and the skill fades.

## An example

The accounts team at a small accounting firm spends hours explaining unmatched items in bank reconciliations. Their upskilling uses that exact task. Each accounts assistant learns to give AI the bank lines and ledger entries and ask it to suggest likely matches and draft explanations. They then check every suggestion against the statement and the ledger before posting anything. The training ends with the workflow in use, not a certificate.

## Upskilling vs reskilling

Upskilling helps someone do their current job better as it changes. Reskilling prepares someone for a different job. With AI, most teams need upskilling first, because the work stays the same but how it gets done changes.
