The best AI automation examples for small teams are the dull ones. Each takes a task someone already repeats every week, such as sorting messages, reading documents or writing a first draft, and hands the repetitive part to AI while a person checks the result. Below are examples for recruitment teams, marketing agencies, and trading and logistics firms, then a way to choose your first one.

These are common patterns, not client stories or results. Whether one fits depends on your tools, your data and how your team works today.

## What makes a task worth automating?

A task is a good candidate when it annoys the person who does it. It should also be safe to check. If a wrong answer would be caught in seconds by a person reading it, you can start. If it would go straight to a customer or into your accounts, keep a person in the loop or leave it alone.

Leaving the task as it is has a cost too: the same hours go into the same copying and sorting every week, and they come out of time your team could spend on work that needs a person. ## What are AI automation examples for recruitment teams?

- **First-pass CV summaries.** AI reads each CV and writes a short summary against the job brief, so a recruiter scans five lines instead of two pages. The recruiter still decides who moves forward.
- **Interview scheduling messages.** AI drafts the reply to a candidate with the available slots, and the recruiter approves it.
- **Job advert drafts.** A hiring manager's notes become a first draft in your house style, ready for editing.

## What are AI automation examples for marketing agencies?

- **Meeting notes into client updates.** AI turns call notes into a draft recap with action items. The account manager removes anything sensitive and sends it.
- **First drafts of repeat content.** Monthly reports, social post variations and campaign summaries start from a draft instead of a blank page.
- **Brief checks.** AI compares an incoming client brief with your usual checklist and lists the questions still open.

## What are AI automation examples for trading and logistics firms?

- **Reading supplier documents.** Invoices, packing lists and shipping documents arrive as PDFs or photos. AI pulls the key fields into a sheet, and a person checks anything that looks odd.
- **Sorting the shared inbox.** AI labels each email as a quote request, delivery query or payment question and routes it to the right person.
- **Drafting status replies.** When a customer asks where an order is, AI drafts a reply from your tracking record for a team member to review.

## What does one of these look like in practice?

Take a five-person trading firm with one inbox for quote requests, delivery questions and supplier emails. Today, whoever sees a message first reads it, decides who should handle it and forwards it.

A simple automation reads each new email, labels its type and assigns it to the usual owner. For quote requests it also drafts a reply in your standard wording. The owner opens an email that is already sorted, reads the draft, edits it and sends it. Nothing goes out until a person presses send.

The people who use the inbox should help choose the labels and the owners. They know which messages are tricky, and they are the ones who will keep it running.

## How do you choose your first one?

1. List the tasks your team repeats every week.
2. Pick the one that costs the most time and is easy to check.
3. Write down the steps a person follows today, including the exceptions.
4. Decide where a person must review the output.
5. Try it on a small batch before it touches real customers.

Start with one task. A single workflow your team actually uses is worth more than five nobody trusts.
