How to measure the ROI of AI training comes down to one question: did the work change afterwards? Four checks answer it, in order: was it relevant, can people do the task without help, are they still using it weeks later, and did a number move. Set that number against what the training cost, and that is the return.

## What do you need before the training starts?

One baseline. You can only show a change if you recorded the start, so before day one, pick a single task, time it a few times and write the figure down. Without that baseline, the fourth check becomes an opinion. Everything below assumes you have it.

## Why can a good course still fail?

The [Singapore Digital Economy Report 2026](https://www.imda.gov.sg/assets/8e4d9951-bc53-4612-80ba-77ee4feaa6da), published by the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) on 5 October 2026, found that 36.7% of surveyed workers had attended AI-related training or courses in the past 12 months ([HRM Asia's summary](https://hrmasia.com/most-employees-in-singapore-now-use-ai-at-work-but-fewer-than-four-in-10-have-been-trained-in-it/)). Attending is not the same as applying.

The same coverage reports employers' own assessment for 2025: at AI-adopting organisations, 47.6% of employees were judged to use AI only for general tasks, and 19.3% to apply it to specific problems in their own domain. These come from different groups, so we do not set one figure against the other. Our reading is that using AI in general and using it on the actual job are different results, and a course can deliver the first without the second. For the skills behind that gap, see [the AI skills Singapore employers want](/insights/ai-skills-singapore-employers-want). For the common reasons, see [why AI courses fade](/insights/why-ai-courses-fade).

## How to tell if an AI course worked: what are the four checks?

A well-known way to separate these results is the Kirkpatrick model, which looks at reaction, learning, behaviour and results ([Kirkpatrick Partners](https://www.kirkpatrickpartners.com/the-kirkpatrick-model/)). Here it is in plain terms for an AI course.

| Check | When | The evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Relevant | The day itself | Each person names a task to try next week |
| 2. Can do it alone | Within a week | A real task done without the trainer, and reviewed |
| 3. Still doing it | 30 to 90 days | Workflows in use, against people trained |
| 4. A number moved | One quarter | Time or errors on one task, before and after |

### 1. Was it relevant? (the day itself)

Ask each person to name one task they will try next week. A named task is evidence. "Great session" is a mood. If most people cannot name a task, the examples were too general.

### 2. Can they do it alone? (within a week)

Give each person a real task from their own job and ask them to do it without the trainer. Look at the output, not the confidence. Check that a person reviews the result before it goes out.

### 3. Are they still doing it? (30 to 90 days)

This is where a course can fail quietly. Look at what is actually in use: which workflows, how often, and by whom. Ask the team lead as well as the attendees. If ten people attended and two still use it, the course reached two.

### 4. Did a number move? (one quarter)

Pick one measure tied to one task, such as minutes per quote, drafts per week or errors caught before sending. Turn saved time into dollars using what that person's time costs you. Report revenue effects separately, as upside. Then work out the return: the saving minus what the training cost, divided by what it cost.

## A worked example

This is an illustration with made-up numbers, not a client story. A recruitment agency has six consultants who each write about five candidate summaries a week. Before the course, they time five summaries and find it takes about 25 minutes each.

The training uses their own candidates. A workflow drafts the summary from the CV and interview notes, and the consultant edits it before it goes to the client. At 30 days the team checks who still uses it, and re-times the task.

Suppose it now takes 15 minutes. That is 10 minutes saved across about 30 summaries a week, or five hours a week for the team. Multiply by what a consultant hour costs the agency and you have a number to put in front of the boss. If only two of the six consultants still use the workflow, the same check shows the saving is smaller, and why.

## If you have been asked whether a course worked

If you are in HR or learning and development, bring three things back to your boss:

- How many people were trained, and how many are still using it.
- One before-and-after figure on a named task.
- What you would change next time.

Better still, ask any provider before you buy: what will you measure, and when? The answer shows whether they plan to look past the classroom door.
