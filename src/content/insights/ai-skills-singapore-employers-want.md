The AI skills Singapore employers want come down to five: applying AI to a specific task in your own field, checking what it produces, handling data responsibly, reworking how a task is done, and the core skills of problem-solving and communication. There is no official list; these five are our reading of the published data.

## What is the gap employers describe?

In its [Singapore Digital Economy Report 2026](https://www.imda.gov.sg/assets/8e4d9951-bc53-4612-80ba-77ee4feaa6da), released on 5 October 2026, the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) reports that AI-adopting firms said on average 71.9% of their workers could use AI in some capacity in 2025. But 47.6% of workers were seen as using it only for general tasks. Only 19.3% were seen as applying AI to specific problems within their domain.

In the report's worker survey, 85.7% said they use AI at work; the 71.9% above is employers' estimate. The skill the data shows is less common is using AI on specific work in your own field.

## Which five skills does the evidence support?

### 1. Applying AI to a task in your own field

This is the domain-specific level above. Among AI-using workers, [the report found](https://www.imda.gov.sg/assets/8e4d9951-bc53-4612-80ba-77ee4feaa6da) content creation was the most common use (76.7%). In the same 2026 survey, only around a quarter regularly use AI to automate tasks or build it into their workflows.

### 2. Checking the output

About half of AI-using workers said they frequently fact-check AI outputs ([IMDA, 2026](https://www.imda.gov.sg/assets/8e4d9951-bc53-4612-80ba-77ee4feaa6da)). The other half do so less often. For an employer, checking is what makes AI output safe to send to a client.

### 3. Using data responsibly

Singapore's Personal Data Protection Commission published [advisory guidelines on the use of personal data in generative AI](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/organisations/regulations-decisions/regulatory-guidance/advisory-guidelines-on-use-of-personal-data-in-generative-ai) on 20 July 2026. Jobstreet's AI Gauge, as reported by [FutureCIO](https://futurecio.tech/demand-for-ai-skills-in-singapore-more-than-triples/) on 27 October 2026, also found rising demand for AI ethics and governance skills in job ads. These sources show rising attention to data and AI governance; we treat this as a skill in its own right. Knowing what client data may go into which tool is part of it.

### 4. Redesigning how a task is done

The Ministry of Manpower's report on AI adoption among firms, published on 30 April 2026, found 18.9% of firms redesigning roles and 13.9% creating new AI-related jobs, and headed its key finding "AI is augmenting but not replacing labour" ([MOM, 30 April 2026](https://mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2026/0430-adoption-of-ai-among-firms)). MOM does not name this a skill; it is our inference that someone must rework the task with AI in the middle.

### 5. The core skills that stay the same

Singapore's skills agency (SSG) said on 27 January 2026 that employers continue to strongly seek problem-solving, collaboration and communication. It also estimated that about a quarter of work tasks can be significantly aided by AI ([SSG](https://www.ssg.gov.sg/newsroom/skillsfuture-singapore-tightens-course-funding-guidelines-and-identifies-skills-trends-to-strengthen-workforce-development/)). AI changes the task. Judgement about what to do with the result stays with the person.

## Is this the same as AI skills in tech job ads?

No, and the difference matters if you are not in a technical role. [IMDA reports](https://www.imda.gov.sg/assets/8e4d9951-bc53-4612-80ba-77ee4feaa6da) that the share of tech job postings requiring at least one AI skill rose from 14.0% in 2024 to 16.7% in 2025. Those are specialised skills such as machine learning and natural language processing. The report defines them as skills for the development, deployment and management of AI systems, not for using AI in an operations, HR or sales role. For most people, skills one to five above are the ones to build.

## A worked example

This is an illustration, not a client story. A recruitment coordinator is asked to use AI for candidate outreach. A general course would teach prompts, which is the general-task level.

A useful skills list for that role is narrower. Draft outreach from a brief in a few minutes. Check the facts about each candidate before sending. Know which candidate details may not go into a public tool. Suggest one change to how the team handles outreach. Each line is something a manager can observe, so progress can be checked against it.

## How do you build your own AI skills list?

Start with your tasks, not a skills catalogue. List the five tasks you repeat most. Next to each, mark whether you use AI for it, use it with checks, or have rebuilt the task around it. The gaps are what to learn next. You can share this list with your manager as it stands.
