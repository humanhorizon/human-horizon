A certificate shows that one person passed an assessment on a defined body of knowledge. It does not show that the team around them now works differently. When weighing AI certification in Singapore for a team, treat the certificate as one useful input, and ask what will change in the work once the course is over.

## What does an AI certificate prove?

It proves that a person met the standard the provider set, on the day they sat the assessment. That is worth something: it is a common reference point, it is easy to put on a CV, and it shows the person put in the effort.

What it cannot prove is how the person works on Monday. A pass shows what someone knows about AI in general. It does not tell an employer whether the team's quotes, reports or client updates are now produced a better way.

## What do recognised AI certification routes assess?

We read three official pages on 16 November 2026. They are examples, not a ranking and not a recommendation.

**A vendor exam.** [AWS Certified AI Practitioner](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-ai-practitioner/) is described by AWS as foundational. It has 65 questions in 90 minutes and is valid for 3 years. According to its [exam guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/ai-practitioner-01/ai-practitioner-01.html), the content is split into five domains, from fundamentals of AI and machine learning (20% of scored content) to applications of foundation models (28%) and guidelines for responsible AI (14%). The guide says the target candidate "uses but does not necessarily build" AI solutions on AWS. It also lists tasks the candidate is not expected to perform, including developing and implementing governance frameworks and policies. On our reading, it tests what a person knows about AI on AWS, and nothing in the guide says it tests whether they can change how a team does its work.

**A polytechnic course on the national directory.** The [Modular Certificate in Artificial Intelligence from Temasek Polytechnic](https://courses.myskillsfuture.gov.sg/courses/TGS-2021008655--Modular-Certificate-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Parttime-Diploma-Infocomm-Digital-Media-Applied-Artificial-Intelligence) is listed on Singapore's national course directory. The listing says it covers developing chatbots, natural language processing and object recognition. That is technical building work. The listing does not describe how the course is assessed.

**A statement of attainment.** Some courses on the same directory end in a statement of attainment issued by the national skills agency. The listing for a [personal data protection practitioner course](https://courses.myskillsfuture.gov.sg/courses/TGS-2023039014) shows the pattern: participants receive it "upon successful completion of the course and assessment". That is a data protection course, not an AI one, so we use it only to show how such a statement works. Check each AI course's own listing for what it assesses, as the AI listing above did not say.

## What does a certificate not show?

Our reading, from the three pages above: the AWS exam tests what an individual knows, the polytechnic listing describes what an individual learns to build, and the third listing describes a statement given to the individual who completes a course and its assessment. None of them, as described, assesses whether a team's workflow changed afterwards.

That fits the pattern we describe for AI training generally. A course can go well in the room and still fade at the desk, because the work the person returns to is the same. We set out the reasons, and what helps, in [why AI courses for employees fade](/insights/why-ai-courses-fade). In short: training holds when people practise on their own tasks, the new way is built into the job, and someone checks that it holds.

## A worked example

This one is illustrative, not a client. A finance team of eight sends two people to sit a foundational AI exam. Both pass. The team still prepares month-end summaries by copying figures from three reports into a template, because nobody has changed that process. The two certificate holders use AI for their own drafting. The other six do not. Six months later the certificate is real, and the team's way of working has not moved.

Now the alternative: the team spends a session building the month-end summary with AI doing the first draft and a person checking the figures. The certificate is optional. The change is already part of the job.

## How should you use certification when choosing for a team?

Ask four questions of any course or exam:

1. What does the assessment test: recall, or doing a real task?
2. Does it cover the tools and data your team actually uses?
3. What changes in the team's workflow afterwards, and who checks the output?
4. How will you know, in a quarter, whether it held?

A certificate for your own record or CV is a fine thing to take. For whoever is responsible for how a team works, the last three questions matter more than the first.
