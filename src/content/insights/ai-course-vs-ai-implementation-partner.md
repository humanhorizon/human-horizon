Choose an AI course if one or a few people want to build their own skills. Choose an AI implementation partner if a specific piece of work should run differently across a team, with someone accountable until it does. The deciding question is whether your problem is knowledge or the way the work gets done.

|  | AI course | AI training and implementation partner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | Individuals | A repeated task and culture of AI adoption across a team |
| You get | Skills each person applies alone | A built and highly customised workflow, a trained team and a before-and-after measure for improved collaboration |
| Wins when | The need is general and people have time | Work must change and nobody has time to build it |
| Usually costs | Less | More (our expectation, not a quoted price) |

## Why the choice feels hard

Both can be described as "AI for your team" but they are different purchases. In the [Ministry of Manpower's report on AI adoption among firms](https://mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2026/0430-adoption-of-ai-among-firms), published on 30 April 2026, high implementation costs (44.9%) and lack of in-house expertise (42.4%) are the most commonly cited constraints. A partner usually costs more than a course and mainly addresses the expertise gap.

Neither is better in general. It depends on what you need.

## When sending your employees on an AI course is the right choice

- Individuals want to get comfortable with AI tools, and they will decide for themselves how to use them.
- You are still exploring and want a feel for what AI can do before committing to anything.
- The people attending have the time and the authority to change how their own work is done.
- The need is general, such as writing, research or spreadsheets, and not tied to one process or how the business works.

A good course is a sensible way to learn. Our guide to [choosing an AI course in Singapore](/ai-course-singapore) sets out what to look for.

## When an AI implementation partner is the right choice

- A repeated task, such as quotes, reports or client updates, takes a team real hours each week.
- Several people do similar work, and you want them to do it the same way.
- You want workflows and processes customised for your team, department or business.
- Nobody inside has the time to build and test the new way, to make AI adoption a culture.
- You want proof afterwards of whether it saved time, in hours and dollars.

A partner does the building with you. They find the tasks worth changing, build the workflow with AI in the middle and a person checking the result, train the people who will use it, and check whether it held. The training is part of the work, not a separate event.

## Five questions that point to the right choice

1. **Is the goal a skill or a changed process?** A skill points to a course. A changed process points to a partner.
2. **How many people do the same task?** One or two can learn it alone. Several people doing similar work will gain from changing together.
3. **Who will build the new way?** If the answer is "whoever finds time", expect it to stall.
4. **How will you know it worked?** If you cannot measure the task today, ask for a before-and-after measure, from either option.
5. **Who looks after it afterwards?** AI tools change often. Someone needs to keep the new way current.

## A worked example

A recruitment agency with 25 staff spends hours each week writing candidate summaries for clients, copying details from CVs and interview notes into a template.

Option one: three consultants attend a course separately. They come back knowing how to write good prompts. Each tries something on their own, in their own style, when they have a quiet hour. The summaries are still written by hand most weeks.

Option two: a partner takes the team's last month of summaries and builds one workflow. AI drafts the summary from the CV and notes, and the consultant edits it before it goes to the client. All the consultants are trained on that workflow, using their own real candidates. The team times a summary before and after. Because client details are involved, they also agree which data may go into which tools first.

Both are reasonable. Which one fits depends on whether the agency wants three people to learn something or all the consultants to work differently.

## If you have been asked to compare options

If you are in HR, training or operations and your management wants a recommendation, ask every provider, course or partner, the same questions to help with decision making:

- What will we have at the end that we did not have before?
- Will you use our own tasks and documents?
- How will we know whether it worked?
- What happens in the second month?
- What will you do with our data?

## Where to start

Write down one task your team repeats, how long it takes and who does it. If a course would solve it, take the course. If the work and the way of working itself has to change, talk to a partner.
